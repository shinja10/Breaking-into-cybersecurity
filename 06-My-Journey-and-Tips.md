# 06 – My Journey and Tips 🚀💻

Cybersecurity isn’t just a career; it’s a journey of curiosity, persistence, and constant learning. Here’s my story and the lessons I’ve learned along the way, along with tips for anyone looking to start their own path.

---

## My Journey

I discovered my passion for cybersecurity in lockdown when everything was shut and I had nothing to do but to scroll through instagram and play games with random people! 
Through curiosity, I wanted to understand **how systems work, how they can be hacked, and how to secure them**.  

Over time, my journey took me through several stages:

### 1. **Learning the Basics**
- I started with **understanding networks, Linux commands, and basic programming**.  
- Tools like **Nmap, Wireshark, and Burp Suite** became my playground for experimentation.  
- I spent hours on platforms like **TryHackMe, Hack The Box, and OverTheWire**, solving challenges and CTFs to build real-world skills.  

**Tip:** Don’t rush. Start with fundamentals—networking, OS basics, and simple scripting. These are the building blocks of cybersecurity.

---

### 2. **Hands-On Labs & Vulnerability Research**
- I began doing **hands-on vulnerability research**, exploring APIs, mobile apps, and web security.  
- I explored **OWASP API security, mobile security testing guides (MSTG), and PortSwigger labs**, documenting my learnings.  
- One memorable moment was completing my **first API security lab**, understanding mass assignment vulnerabilities and how improper authorization can be exploited.  

**Tip:** Labs are your best friends. The more hands-on you are, the faster you learn. Don’t just read, **practice**!!

---

### 3. **Mobile Security Exploration**
- I delved into **iOS and Android security**, learning about **insecure data storage, permissions issues, and insecure communication**.  
- I followed the **MSTG guides**, combined with YouTube tutorials, to test apps in simulators and emulators.  
- This experience taught me the importance of **thinking like an attacker while protecting the user’s data**.  

**Tip:** Mobile security is a growing field. Start testing apps in a safe environment—use emulators, your own devices, and follow official guides.

---

### 4. **Professional Experience**
- I started with **Hackshield**, an app for device security! I learned Web App and mobile App basics and worked on my corporate skills.
- I joined **KPMG as an associate consultant**, applying my skills in real-world environments.  
- During this time, I learned about **corporate security standards, audits, and compliance**, balancing theory with practical execution.  
- I also co-managed my **family’s sugar business**, gaining skills in management, decision-making, and handling responsibility.

**Tip:** Any professional experience, even outside cybersecurity, adds value. Soft skills like communication, teamwork, and problem-solving are as important as technical skills.

---

### 5. **Community & Teaching**
- I became a **core team member of Cyber Secured India**, a non-profit promoting cybersecurity awareness.  
- Conducting **training sessions, workshops, and talks** helped me solidify my knowledge and learn to explain complex topics in simple ways.  
- Sharing what you learn is a powerful way to grow—not just for others, but for yourself.
- The **positive feedback** from these sessions was motivating and encouraged me to keep learning and teaching.
- I now aim to **mentor more students and organize additional workshops**, giving back to the cybersecurity community.  


**Tip:** Teach what you learn. Blogging, YouTube videos, or talks help retain knowledge and build a professional profile.

---

### 6. **Mental Health, Burnouts & Imposter Syndrome**
- Cybersecurity is exciting but can be overwhelming. I faced **burnout during intense lab sessions and professional projects**.  
- At times, I also struggled with **imposter syndrome**, feeling like I wasn’t “good enough” despite making real progress.  
- I learned to **take breaks, balance work and learning, and celebrate small wins**. Mindset matters as much as skill.  
- Practicing self-care, exercising, and connecting with supportive communities helped me manage stress and stay motivated.  

**Tip:** Don’t compare yourself to others. Focus on **your own growth**. Mental health is critical in a field that demands constant learning.

---
### 7. **Balancing Life & Learning**
- My journey hasn’t always been smooth. My mother’s health issues required me to **balance family responsibilities and work**, teaching me discipline and time management.  
- I also learned **financial planning**, saving for my Master’s in the UK while continuing to build my skills.  

**Tip:** Cybersecurity requires focus, but don’t forget life balance. Plan your finances, time, and personal growth alongside learning.

---

## Lessons Learned & Advice for Beginners

1. **Start Small:** Don’t aim to master everything at once. Focus on fundamentals first.  
2. **Hands-On Practice is Key:** Platforms like TryHackMe, Hack The Box, PortSwigger labs, and MSTG are invaluable.  
3. **Document Your Journey:** Keep notes, GitHub repos, or blogs. It helps with interviews and personal growth.  
4. **Learn from Real-World Scenarios:** Read incident reports, CTF write-ups, or security blogs to see how vulnerabilities are exploited.  
5. **Networking Matters:** Join communities, attend webinars, and talk to other security professionals.  
6. **Balance Theory & Practice:** Certifications are important, but practical skills make you stand out.  
7. **Persistence is Everything:** You will fail sometimes—but failure is how you learn. Keep pushing.

---

> 💡 **Final Thought:**  
> Cybersecurity is not just about tools or certifications—it’s a mindset. If something can be hacked, you should want to understand **why, how, and how to prevent it**. Be curious, be patient, and keep learning. Your journey starts with the first small step, and every day of practice brings you closer to mastery.
